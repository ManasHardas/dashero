# Outreach — the one-week test before any code

**Goal:** find out whether people who send the same report to many clients every month would pay for
a simpler, cheaper way. Not to sell. To find out.

**Deadline:** replies in by **2026-09-12**. Fewer than 5 people saying they'd pay means stop.

---

## Do this first — it takes an hour

Two questions decide whether the outreach is even worth sending:

1. **Does DashThis do per-client fan-out with branding?** They charge per dashboard ($429 for 50) and
   advertise "we don't charge per client". If they already do branded per-recipient reports, the
   price advantage shrinks to almost nothing.
2. **How much does AgencyAnalytics' Google Sheets connection actually do?** They list Sheets as a
   source. If it's a full connector, the "your data is already in a sheet" angle is much weaker than
   it looks.

If both come back "yes, they already do this well" — stop here. Don't send anything.

---

## Who to contact — about 50 people, in three groups

**Group A — freelancers who build these reports for money (~25 people).**
PeoplePerHour lists 300+ people doing Google Sheets dashboard work. Profiles are public and show
completed-project counts. Sort by that count and take the top 25 — someone with 100+ completed jobs
has repeat clients, which is the whole point. Someone bidding $10 a job does not.

**Group B — small agencies (~15 people).**
AgencyAnalytics and DashThis publish customer stories naming real agencies. Also r/PPC, r/agency and
r/DigitalMarketing, where "what's everyone using for client reporting" threads appear regularly.
**Target small ones — 5 to 15 clients.** An agency with 40 clients will not switch; rebuilding 40
client reports is a genuinely awful week. An agency with 6 clients has almost no switching cost.

**Group C — people who complained recently (~10 people).**
Anyone in the last year who posted about client reporting taking too long, about maintaining too many
dashboard copies, or about moving off a paid tool. These are the warmest contacts available and they
have already described the problem in their own words.

## Message 1 — for freelancers (Group A)

> **Subject:** quick question about your Sheets dashboard work
>
> Hi [name] — I saw you've done [N] dashboard projects, and it looks like most of them are Google
> Sheets.
>
> I'm looking into building a tool for people who do this work over and over, and I'd rather ask than
> guess. Nothing is built yet, so I'm not selling you anything.
>
> Two questions:
>
> 1. When a client wants the same report every month, what does that actually look like for you now —
>    do you rebuild it, or do you have a system?
> 2. What's the most annoying part? The data, making the charts, or getting it to the client?
>
> Happy to pay for 20 minutes if that's easier than typing.
>
> [your name]

## Message 2 — for agencies (Group B)

> **Subject:** how do you handle client reporting?
>
> Hi [name] — you run [agency]. Quick question, not a pitch.
>
> I'm looking at building something for agencies that send the same report to a lot of clients every
> month. Nothing exists yet — I'm trying to work out whether the problem is real before I build
> anything.
>
> If you're on AgencyAnalytics, DashThis, or Looker Studio right now: what made you pick it, and
> what still annoys you about it?
>
> Genuinely just want to know. Happy to send you what I learn from everyone else.
>
> [your name]

## Message 3 — for people who complained (Group C)

> Hi [name] — you posted [month] about [their exact problem, in their words].
>
> I'm looking at building something for this and I'd rather talk to people who've actually hit it
> than guess. Nothing built yet, nothing to sell.
>
> Is that still a problem for you? And what did you end up doing about it?
>
> [your name]

## Rules for sending

- **Send them one at a time, personalised.** A visible mail merge gets deleted. Twenty real messages
  beat two hundred templated ones.
- **Never claim the product exists.** "Nothing is built yet" is why people reply — you're asking for
  help, not selling.
- **Don't describe the solution in the first message.** You want their description of the problem
  before you contaminate it with yours. If they ask what you're building, then tell them.
- **Ask about what they do now, not what they'd like.** People are unreliable about what they want and
  accurate about what they already do.
- **Send about 20 in week one**, see what comes back, then adjust before sending the rest.

## What to listen for

**Good signs.** They describe the problem before you do. They name a number — how many clients, how
many hours a month. They already pay for something. They ask what you're building.

**Bad signs.** "Sounds useful" with no detail. They use free Looker Studio and are happy. They have
one or two clients. Nothing but politeness.

**The thing that would kill it.** If most say some version of *"yeah it's annoying but I've got a
workaround"* — that's the market telling you it will admire this and never buy it. That is the
single most likely outcome and the whole reason to send these before building anything.

---

## Then, and only then — the money question

Once about 10 people have replied and described the problem in their own words, go back to the ones
who were most specific and ask directly:

> Based on what you told me, here's what I'd build: [two sentences]. It would be $99/month flat for
> up to 50 clients — you're paying [$X] now. If I had it working in six weeks, would you pay for it?
> I'm asking for a real yes or no, not encouragement.

**Five real yeses by 12 September and it's worth building. Fewer than five and it isn't.**

Asking for money in a first cold email doesn't work. Asking after someone has spent ten minutes
explaining their problem to you does.
